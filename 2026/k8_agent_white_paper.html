Abstract

This white paper proposes a hybrid remediation framework for Kubernetes-based services operated at scale. The framework replaces brittle “if-then” automation and undocumented tribal workflows with a structured runbook-as-data model that supports deterministic triage, evidence-linked diagnosis, policy-guarded remediation proposals, explicit contraindications, and human approvals. Large language models (LLMs) are used only to summarize evidence and provide explainability, never to make authoritative decisions or execute actions. The system of record for evidence, diagnoses, actions, outcomes, and audit is Databricks and Salesforce.

Motivation

In large clusters and managed services, alert fatigue is caused not only by alert volume but by decision complexity. A non-ready pod is a symptom. The root cause may be scheduling, DNS, storage, configuration drift, resource limits, upstream service failures, or node pressure. Traditional monitoring surfaces signals but does not provide a safe, explainable, auditable path from deviation to reconciliation. Human responders rely on institutional knowledge and interpret runbooks under stress, leading to slower recovery, inconsistent remediation, and weak audit traceability.

Design Goals

  • Safety first: hard guardrails and contraindications must be non-bypassable.
  • Deterministic authority: decisions and execution paths must be explainable and reproducible.
  • Human-in-the-loop for risk: structural changes and protected scopes require explicit approval.
  • Runbooks as data: operational knowledge is structured, versioned, testable, and queryable.
  • Evidence-based explainability: every diagnosis and action links to concrete evidence and policy version.
  • Extensibility: support multiple resource kinds and managed services without rewriting the platform.
  • Enterprise alignment: store truth and workflow state in Databricks and Salesforce.

Non-Goals

  • Fully autonomous remediation in production by default.
  • Replacing observability or incident management platforms.
  • Using LLMs as authoritative decision-makers.

Design Thesis: Why a Clinical Model?

Production remediation is a high-risk, often irreversible decision domain. Medicine is the most mature system humanity has built for managing uncertainty, safety, consent, and audit at scale. Existing AIOps tools primarily optimize detection and correlation; this framework deliberately optimizes safe decision-making and accountability.

In practice, this is implemented through a runbook-as-data model that encodes symptoms (signals), differential diagnosis, confirmatory diagnostics, contraindications, approval requirements, and an immutable audit trail linking every action to evidence and policy version.

Core Concept: Runbooks as Data

In this framework, a runbook is not a document and not a script. A runbook is a normalized set of structured artifacts describing: (1) what failures exist (pathologies), (2) what symptoms indicate them (signals), (3) what tests confirm them (diagnostics), (4) what actions are permitted (therapeutics), (5) where actions are forbidden or require approvals (contraindications), and (6) how success is verified (post-op verification). Operational behavior emerges by evaluating these artifacts against evidence and constraints.

System Overview

The framework is organized into three logical layers with a closed-loop feedback mechanism: (1) a versioned Policy Library (static knowledge), (2) an Execution Record (dynamic case charts), and (3) a governed Mutation Engine (controlled executor). LLMs may generate clinical summaries and explanations, but deterministic policy enforcement and approvals control execution.

Layer 1: Policy Library (Static Knowledge, Versioned)

This layer defines “medical knowledge” as structured artifacts. Changes are proposed via GitOps or controlled publishing and are reviewable and testable.

  • Lexicon (Pathology Catalog): known failure modes with severity and default priority.
  • Triage Signals: symptom definitions (regex, JSONPath, metric thresholds) with weights.
  • Diagnostics: permissioned confirmatory tests and expected confirmation criteria.
  • Pathophysiology: dependency and complication mappings to estimate blast radius and cascading risk.
  • Therapeutics: verified action catalog categorized by risk (observational, transient, structural).
  • Contraindications: non-bypassable safety constraints and approval requirements by scope.
  • Verification Specs: post-treatment checks to validate stabilization and detect regression.

Layer 2: Execution Record (Dynamic Case Chart)

This layer records real-time triage and remediation lifecycle for a “patient” (unreconciled object). Records are immutable where possible and always evidence-linked.

  • Admissions: intake queue of failing objects (namespace/name/UID, timestamps, status).
  • Observed Signals: symptom detections with signal identifiers and evidence pointers.
  • Differential Scores: deterministic ranking of candidate pathologies based on signal weights and confirmations.
  • Clinical Notes: LLM-generated summaries/explanations referencing evidence IDs (explicitly non-authoritative).
  • Treatment Plans: ranked candidate therapeutics constrained by contraindications and approval tiers.
  • Mutation Runs: execution records for actions that proceed, including approvals and verification results.
  • Outcomes: stability windows, success/failure, regression markers for continuous improvement.

Layer 3: Mutation Engine (Governed Remediation Orchestrator)

The Mutation Engine executes only after deterministic validation and governance gates:

  1. Gather evidence and compute differential diagnosis deterministically.
  2. Map diagnosis to candidate therapeutics.
  3. Evaluate contraindications and approval requirements.
  4. If blocked, record the reason and optionally open a human workflow.
  5. If allowed and approved, execute via verified actions.
  6. Perform post-op verification.
  7. Record outcomes and update admission state.

Decision Authority Split (Safety Rule)

  • LLM authority: summarization and narrative explanation only; no execution authority.
  • Deterministic authority: policy evaluation, contraindications, approvals, and execution gating.
  • Human authority: required for protected scopes and high-risk structural changes; approval is recorded as data.

Practical rule: If an action cannot be justified using evidence references and policy versions stored in the system of record, it must not execute.

System of Record: Databricks and Salesforce

The system stores truth and workflow state in Databricks and Salesforce. Databricks supports scalable evidence storage and analytics (what happened, what worked, what repeats). Salesforce supports enterprise workflow, approvals, case tracking, and an Agentforce interface layer.

Databricks stores

  • Evidence references and structured evidence payloads
  • Admissions, observed signals, differential scores, treatment plans, mutation runs, outcomes
  • Policy snapshots and policy version references for audit

Salesforce stores

  • Approval workflows (who approved, when, why) and case management for admissions requiring human action
  • Agentforce Topic definitions and controlled actions integration points

End-to-End Workflow

  1. Admission: A failing resource is admitted based on deterministic triggers (events/status/metrics/alerts).
  2. Triage: Evaluate triage signals; record observed signals with evidence references.
  3. Differential diagnosis: Compute ranked candidate pathologies deterministically.
  4. Diagnostics: Execute confirmatory tests when indicated and allowed; store outputs as evidence.
  5. Treatment planning: Propose ranked therapeutics with risk and required approval tier.
  6. Contraindications: Block disallowed actions; record explicit reasons.
  7. Approval: If required, route through Salesforce approval workflow.
  8. Execution: Execute only after policy and approval gates; record exact change and context.
  9. Verification: Validate stabilization using verification specs; detect regressions.
  10. Outcome: Record success/failure and close admission; use outcomes to refine the policy library.

Policy Versioning and Contribution Model

The Policy Library is treated as versioned content:

  • Each pathology, signal, diagnostic, therapeutic, contraindication, and verification spec has an identifier and version metadata.
  • Changes are proposed via GitOps or controlled publishing and reviewed by maintainers.
  • Each deployed policy snapshot is immutable and referenced by runtime records for audit.
  • Testing includes schema validation and guardrail checks to prevent unsafe policy updates.

Open-Source Readiness

The framework can be open-sourced at the level of schemas and contribution workflow: runbook-as-data formats, validation rules, and reference (dry-run / read-only) logic. Enterprise execution wiring, tenant policies, approval rules, and incident outcomes remain private to preserve security, compliance, and differentiation.

Security and Compliance Considerations

  • Least privilege for diagnostics and executors; deny-by-default for structural actions.
  • Explicit allowlists for permitted diagnostics and actions; command and action surfaces are controlled.
  • Immutable audit records linking evidence, diagnosis, policy version, approvals, and outcomes.
  • Data governance in Databricks and Salesforce aligned with enterprise access controls and retention.

Implementation Roadmap Alignment (Q1–Q3)

  • Q1: Read-only admission/triage/diagnosis with clinical notes and full audit foundations (no mutations).
  • Q2: Treatment proposals and human-approved low-risk execution; Salesforce approvals; contraindication enforcement.
  • Q3: Expand coverage for managed services; add blast radius and SLO-aware gating; post-op verification and outcomes; executive metrics.

Conclusion

The Kubernetes Physician-Agent Framework formalizes operational runbooks into a governed, auditable, explainable control plane. By making runbooks structured data and making policy enforcement deterministic, the framework enables safe remediation at scale. LLMs add value as summarizers and explainers without becoming authoritative agents. Using Databricks and Salesforce as the system of record aligns the system with enterprise governance and provides a path to broad adoption.

Appendix A: Example End-to-End Scenario — Neo4j Pod Memory Pressure (Heap Exhaustion)

A.1 Scenario Overview

A Neo4j pod running in Kubernetes begins restarting repeatedly and enters CrashLoopBackOff. Application logs show repeated java.lang.OutOfMemoryError. The service is customer-facing, deployed in a production namespace, and backed by persistent volumes. The immediate risk is prolonged unavailability; the longer-term risk is unsafe, ad-hoc remediation under pressure.

A.2 Knowledge Encoded as Data (Simplified View)

Pathology (Lexicon Entry)

  • pathology_id: PATH-N4J-01
  • name: Neo4j Heap Exhaustion
  • description: JVM heap usage exceeds allocated memory, causing OOMKilled restarts
  • severity: High
  • default_priority: High

Triage Signals

  • signal_id: SIG-N4J-LOG-OOM
    source: container logs; match_type: REGEX; match_value: java.lang.OutOfMemoryError; weight: 0.7
  • signal_id: SIG-POD-OOMKILLED
    source: pod status; match_type: JSONPATH; match_value: lastState.terminated.reason == OOMKilled; weight: 0.8

Diagnostics

  • diagnostic_id: DIAG-N4J-HEAP
    description: inspect heap usage and GC pressure (via cypher-shell or metrics endpoint)
    confirm_condition: sustained heap > 90% with frequent full GCs

Therapeutics (Mutation Catalog)

  • therapeutic_id: TH-N4J-OBSERVE-HEAP
    category: supportive/observational; risk_level: low; action: collect heap/GC metrics and temporary diagnostic logging
  • therapeutic_id: TH-N4J-INCREASE-MEM
    category: surgical/structural; risk_level: high; action: increase JVM heap and Kubernetes memory limits

Contraindications

  • constraint_id: CON-PROD-STRUCTURAL
    scope: namespace = production; applies_to: structural therapeutics; rule: requires explicit human approval
    message: “Structural changes in production require SRE approval”

Post-Op Verification

  • verification_id: VER-N4J-STABLE
    condition: pod remains Ready with stable heap usage for an observation window (e.g., 30 minutes)

A.3 Runtime Execution Steps

  1. Admission: Pod admitted when CrashLoopBackOff/OOMKilled criteria are met; identity and evidence references recorded.
  2. Triage: Signals detected (log OOM + status OOMKilled); observed signals written with evidence pointers.
  3. Differential diagnosis: PATH-N4J-01 ranks highest deterministically based on weighted signals.
  4. Diagnostics: DIAG-N4J-HEAP confirms sustained heap pressure and GC thrash; results stored as evidence.
  5. Clinical note (LLM): short evidence-referenced summary generated; explicitly non-authoritative.
  6. Treatment plan: Option A (observe) and Option B (increase memory) proposed with risk and approval tier.
  7. Contraindication: structural prod change blocked until approval; block reason recorded.
  8. Approval: Salesforce approval requested and granted by on-call SRE with justification recorded.
  9. Execution: memory limit update applied through verified action path; execution record written.
  10. Verification: stability window checks pass; restarts cease and heap normalizes.
  11. Outcome: admission marked resolved; outcome data stored for analytics and policy refinement.

A.4 Governance Outcome

  • Unsafe automatic behavior was prevented by contraindications.
  • Human approval was required and recorded for a high-risk structural change.
  • All decisions were traceable to evidence, policy version, and approver identity.
  • LLM assistance improved clarity but did not influence authority.

A.5 Audit Trail Summary

The system records admission, observed signals, differential diagnosis, diagnostic results, treatment proposals, contraindication blocks, approval metadata, execution details, verification results, and outcome closure. Each record links back to evidence references and policy version identifiers.
